# Types of Bias in AI Outputs

Source: Mark Anderson's "Types of Bias in AI Outputs" infographic (AI Literacy & Critical Thinking series, @ICTEvangelist). Transcribed verbatim for reuse in chapter 3. The guide version will be rewritten for the student audience; this file keeps Mark's original wording as the reference.

## Data & Training biases

- **Historical Bias** — Training data reflects past inequalities, which the model absorbs and perpetuates.
- **Sampling Bias** — Training data over- or under-represents certain groups, skewing model understanding.
- **Measurement Bias** — Inconsistent data collection or labelling across groups introduces systematic errors.
- **Reporting Bias** — What gets documented — and what doesn't — skews the model's view of the world.
- **Recency Bias** — Over-weights recent data, undermining older but still-valid context and knowledge.
- **Popularity Bias** — Favours mainstream content over niche but equally valid perspectives.
- **Annotator Bias** — Human labellers' own values shape what the model learns is "correct".

## Output & Behaviour biases

- **Confirmation Bias** — Reinforces what the user already seems to believe rather than balanced perspectives.
- **Amplification Bias** — Existing biases are exaggerated in outputs — not merely replicated, but intensified.
- **Framing Bias** — How a question is phrased changes the answer in ways that are not neutral.
- **Sycophancy Bias** — The model tells users what they want to hear, prioritising approval over accuracy.
- **Feedback Loop Bias** — User interactions reinforce existing biases through iterative retraining signals.
- **Language Bias** — English-language training dominates, reducing quality for other languages.

## Representation biases

- **Gender Bias** — Associates roles or traits with gender stereotypes embedded in training data.
- **Racial / Ethnic Bias** — Unequal performance or harmful associations across racial and ethnic groups.
- **Geographic Bias** — Over-represents Western, English-language perspectives in knowledge and values.
- **Socioeconomic Bias** — Skews toward dominant economic norms, marginalising lower-income perspectives.
- **Disability Bias** — Outputs often exclude or misrepresent disabled people and their lived experiences.

## Model & Design

- **Algorithmic Bias** — Bias from model architecture or objective function, independent of training data.

## Evaluator & Human

- **Automation Bias** — Outputs are trusted uncritically simply because a machine produced them.
- **Anchoring Bias** — The first AI output disproportionately shapes all subsequent human judgement.
