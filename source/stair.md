# STAIR approach to using GenAI

Source: Mark Anderson, ICT Evangelist graphic (CC licensed). Transcribed verbatim for chapter 6. The published examples are teacher-facing; the guide needs student-facing equivalents (revision, self-quizzing, feedback on drafts) written in the same shape.

- **Specific** — Be precise and specific with your language.
- **Tell** — Tell it exactly what you want it to do in minutiae.
- **Actionable** — Don't just give it one activity, give it the lot and explain what you want.
- **Iterate** — Be prepared for it to not get it right the first time. Iteration is essential!
- **Role** — Help the GenAI know what to do by giving it a role, e.g. Physics professor.

## Mark's example prompts (teacher-facing, for shape reference)

### Retrieval questions

> Act as a primary teacher who is a science specialist. Create retrieval questions on the water cycle for Year 5. Keep tightly to evaporation, condensation, precipitation and collection. Present the output in clearly labelled sections.
>
> Pupils should be able to demonstrate confident explanations and supply short applied questions.
>
> Every question must include plausible distractors based on age-appropriate misconceptions.
>
> Keep all questions concise with no added detail, definitions or answers.

### Quick CFUs

> Act as a primary teacher who is a science specialist and create quick checks for understanding on the water cycle for my highly able Year 5 class.
>
> Keep tightly to the curriculum content of evaporation, condensation, precipitation and collection.
>
> Make each CFU brief and easy for pupils to respond to, using clear wording that maintains the flow of the lesson.
>
> Include a mix of formats, such as short recall prompts, simple multiple-choice checks with plausible distractors where appropriate, and quick sketch or highlight tasks.
>
> Match the level of challenge to my highly able class, keeping the questions concise and free from unnecessary detail, explanations or answers.
