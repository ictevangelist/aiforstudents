**Standfirst:** Before you use it, it helps to know what it is. The real version, not the film version.

Here's a question. When you type something into an AI chatbot and it replies in perfect sentences, what do you think is happening on the other side?

Most people imagine something like a giant brain attached to a giant library. It reads your question, finds the right facts, and writes them up for you.

That's not what's happening. And once you understand what actually is, everything else in this guide will make a lot more sense.

## It's a prediction machine

You already use a small version of this technology every day. When you're typing a message and your phone suggests your next word, that's prediction. Your keyboard has learned which words tend to follow which, and it offers its best guess.

An AI chatbot is that idea scaled up to a size that's hard to imagine. Before you ever met it, it was trained on an enormous amount of human writing: books, websites, articles, conversations. From all of that text it learned the patterns of language. Which words follow which. In what order. In which situations.

So when you ask it something, it doesn't go and look anything up. It builds a reply one word at a time, and each word is chosen because, based on everything it has read, that word is likely to come next.

Caption: It picks what's likely. Nobody checks whether it's true.]

That's really it. Not a mind. Not a search engine. A very powerful pattern machine.

## Likely is not the same as true

This is the single most useful thing to know about AI, so it's worth saying plainly.

The chatbot's job is to produce text that fits the pattern of a good answer. Most of the time, the likeliest words and the correct words are the same thing, which is why it's often right. But the machine has no way of telling the difference. It doesn't know when it's right. It doesn't know when it's wrong. It doesn't know anything at all, in the way you know things.

That's why AI can tell you something completely false in the same calm, confident tone it uses for something completely true. The confidence is part of the pattern. It learned to sound sure from millions of humans who wrote as if they were sure.

We'll spend the whole of chapter 2 on what to do about that.

## It learned from us

One more thing about all that training text: humans wrote it.

That means the machine picked up our best work. Brilliant explanations, careful arguments, beautiful writing. But it also picked up our habits, our assumptions, our blind spots and our prejudices, because those are stitched through human writing too.

An AI doesn't decide to be unfair, any more than it decides to be wrong. It reflects what it was trained on. Keep that thought; it's where chapter 3 begins.

## So what is it good for?

Plenty, when you use it knowing what it is. Because it's a pattern machine, it's genuinely strong at pattern jobs: rephrasing something you don't understand, summarising, translating, suggesting ideas when you're stuck, quizzing you on a topic you're revising.

What it can't do is care whether it's right, know what you were taught in class, or take the blame when it's wrong. You bring the judgement. That's not a limitation to work around. That's the deal.

## 3 takeaways

1. **AI predicts, it doesn't know.** It builds answers word by word based on patterns in human writing.
2. **Confident isn't the same as correct.** It sounds just as sure when it's wrong.
3. **It learned from people.** Our best thinking and our worst habits went in together.
